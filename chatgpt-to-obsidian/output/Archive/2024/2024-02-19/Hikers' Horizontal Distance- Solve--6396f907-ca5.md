# Hikers' Horizontal Distance: Solve

## User

Three hikers are shown crossing a footbridge. Knowing
that the weights of the hikers at points C, D and E are E 
200 N,175 N and 135 N respectively. Determine the 
horizontal distance from A to the line of action of the
resultant of the three weights when a is 3.3 m.

## Assistant

To determine the horizontal distance (X) from point A to the line of action of the resultant of the three weights, you can use the principle of moments. The sum of the clockwise moments must equal the sum of the counterclockwise moments.

Let's denote the distances from A to C, D, and E as \(x_C\), \(x_D\), and \(x_E\) respectively.

The moment equation is given by:

\\[200 \cdot x_C + 175 \cdot x_D + 135 \cdot x_E = 0\\]

You also know that \(x_C + x_D + x_E = 3.3 \) m.

Now you can solve these two equations to find \(x_C, x_D, x_E\) and then find the horizontal distance \(X\) from A to the line of action of the resultant, which is \(X = x_C\).

## Assistant

The line of action of the resultant force is determined by finding the equivalent or resultant force from the combination of individual forces. In this case, we use the principle of moments to find the horizontal distance from point A to the line of action of the resultant force.

The principle of moments states that for an object in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point must be equal to the sum of the counterclockwise moments about the same point.

For the given scenario:

\\[200 \cdot x_C + 175 \cdot x_D + 135 \cdot x_E = 0\\]

This equation ensures that the sum of the clockwise moments (moments created by the weights) is equal to the sum of the counterclockwise moments, indicating equilibrium.

The horizontal distance from A to the line of action of the resultant force is found by determining the value of \(x_C\) (or \(X\)), and it represents the point where the combined effect of the forces can be considered as a single force acting at that distance from A.
