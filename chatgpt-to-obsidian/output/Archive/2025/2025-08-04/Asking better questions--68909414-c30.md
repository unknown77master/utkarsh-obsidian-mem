# Asking better questions

## User

Can you please tell me what are the 5 to 10 ways i can ask better questions compared to the top 1% user on this platform

## Assistant

Certainly, Utkarsh. Asking better questions—especially to a system like me—means getting more **precise**, **structured**, and **context-rich** answers that align closely with your goals. The top 1% of users typically treat interactions like a dialogue with a skilled expert or mentor.

Here are **5 to 10 actionable strategies** to consistently ask better questions than even the top 1% of users:

---

### **1. Be Goal-Oriented, Not Just Curious**
- **Bad:** "Tell me about cloud computing."
- **Better:** "Can you explain how cloud computing supports scalable AI model deployment in production?"

✅ **Tip:** Always start with *why* you're asking. Define the end goal.

---

### **2. Provide Context Like a Researcher**
- **Bad:** "Explain semaphores."
- **Better:** "I'm studying synchronization in OS (Unit III, 2nd year AI&DS). Can you explain semaphores in the context of Producer-Consumer problems using real-world analogies?"

✅ **Tip:** Mention course, project, role, or scenario for relevance.

---

### **3. Define the Format You Want**
- **Bad:** "Give me a summary."
- **Better:** "Summarize this topic in bullet points under 100 words, and then give a 3-line conclusion in formal academic tone."

✅ **Tip:** Guide the structure—list, code block, comparison table, graph, summary, etc.

---

### **4. Ask Follow-Up Like a Scientist**
- **Bad:** "Thanks." 
- **Better:** "How would that change if I used a mutex instead of a semaphore in this case?"

✅ **Tip:** The top users treat responses as hypotheses to test further.

---

### **5. Ask for Comparisons or Trade-offs**
- **Bad:** "What is better—React or Vue?"
- **Better:** "Can you compare React and Vue for building data dashboards with real-time updates and lightweight animations?"

✅ **Tip:** Force comparative thinking to deepen your own understanding.

---

### **6. Use Constraints to Focus**
- **Bad:** "Give me a carnivore diet."
- **Better:** "Design a carnivore diet for a 20 y/o male, 55 kg, 172 cm, red-meat-free, aiming for muscle gain and training 5x/week."

✅ **Tip:** Include budget, time, preferences, or exclusions.

---

### **7. Layer Questions for Multi-Level Insight**
- **Level 1:** "What is mutex?"
- **Level 2:** "Where is mutex preferred over semaphore?"
- **Level 3:** "In which OS kernel subsystems are mutexes predominantly used, and why?"

✅ **Tip:** Start from surface → intermediate → expert level within one prompt.

---

### **8. Use Examples to Disambiguate**
- **Bad:** "Explain normalization."
- **Better:** "I'm confused between 1NF and 2NF. Here's my table—can you show me what needs to change to make it 2NF?"

✅ **Tip:** Attach examples, datasets, or your own trial for clearer answers.

---

### **9. Request Real-World Application**
- **Bad:** "What are semaphores used for?"
- **Better:** "Where are semaphores used in real-world systems like ATM networks or railway systems? Explain with architecture or flow."

✅ **Tip:** Tie abstract theory to real-world systems.

---

### **10. Ask for Mistakes or Misconceptions**
- **Advanced users ask:** 
 - “What are common mistakes students make when learning convolution in CNNs?” 
 - “What are the misconceptions in applying k-means to non-spherical clusters?”

✅ **Tip:** This not only prevents errors but builds meta-cognition.

---

### 💡 Final Thought:
Asking elite-level questions isn’t about being smarter—it's about thinking **intentionally**. Treat every question like you’re preparing for a research paper, an internship interview, or teaching someone else.
