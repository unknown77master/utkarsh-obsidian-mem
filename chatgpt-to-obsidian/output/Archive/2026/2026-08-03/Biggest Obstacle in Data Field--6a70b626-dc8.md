## Assistant

From what I've seen across our conversations, your biggest obstacle is **not your technical ability—it's turning your knowledge into evidence that employers trust.**

Here's how I'd rank the obstacles:

1. **Lack of standout, production-level projects (Biggest obstacle)**
 - You know Python, SQL, machine learning, and data science fundamentals.
 - But many of your projects are academic or proof-of-concept.
 - Companies hiring for AI, ML, or data roles want to see systems that solve real business problems, not just trained models.

2. **Trying to prepare for too many directions at once**
 - You've explored Data Analyst, Data Scientist, AI/ML Engineer, AI Platform Engineer, frontend development, SaaS products, automation, and backend engineering.
 - This broad learning is valuable, but it can dilute your portfolio if your resume doesn't tell one clear story.

3. **Limited professional experience**
 - As an engineering student, you're competing with candidates who may have internships or open-source contributions.
 - Strong projects, GitHub activity, and technical blogs can help bridge that gap.

4. **Consistency**
 - You've mentioned losing discipline after making progress and sometimes feeling pressured by comparisons.
 - Long-term consistency in building, documenting, and applying will matter more than learning another framework.

The good news is that your strengths are aligned with the current market:
- Strong programming foundation.
- Genuine curiosity about modern AI.
- Willingness to build complex projects.
- Interest in learning tools used in industry.

For the kind of AI roles you've been targeting recently, I believe the single biggest thing that would increase your chances is to build **2–3 polished, end-to-end AI/data projects** with deployment, documentation, testing, and measurable impact. Those projects will demonstrate the practical skills employers are looking for far more effectively than additional coursework alone.

What questions should I ask industry professionals

## Assistant

When talking to industry professionals, avoid asking questions that you can easily find online. Instead, ask about their experience and decision-making. Since you're aiming for AI and data roles, these questions can lead to valuable insights:

### Career
- What skills do you use most in your day-to-day work?
- If you were starting your career again today, what would you focus on?
- What separates candidates who get interviews from those who don't?
- What mistakes do you see fresh graduates make during interviews?

### Projects & Portfolio
- What kind of projects make you think, "I'd interview this person"?
- What is one project every aspiring AI/ML engineer should build?
- How important are deployed applications compared to Jupyter notebooks?
- What makes a GitHub profile stand out?

### Technical Skills
- Which tools are used most on your team?
- Which AI or data skills are becoming more important over the next few years?
- How much system design should a fresher know?
- What concepts should I master before learning the latest frameworks?

### Hiring & Interviews
- What are the most common interview questions for fresh graduates?
- What is an immediate red flag on a resume?
- How can a candidate with no internship compete with experienced applicants?
- What would you improve if you reviewed my resume?

### Workplace
- What does a typical week look like in your role?
- What surprised you most after joining the industry?
- What skills did you wish you had learned in college?

### Networking
- What communities, newsletters, or people do you recommend following?
- Is there any advice you wish someone had given you when you were a student?

### End the conversation well
A great closing question is:
> **"Based on what we've discussed, if you were in my position today, what would be the next three things you'd do over the next six months?"**

This often leads to the most practical and personalized advice.

Also, after the conversation, thank them and send a connection request. A simple message like, *"Thank you for sharing your insights today. I learned a lot from our conversation and would love to stay connected,"* leaves a positive impression.
