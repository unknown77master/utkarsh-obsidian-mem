# Scrum Master and Meetings

## Assistant

It sounds like you're asking about **Scrum Master**, **Scrum Call**, and **Stand-up Meeting**. These are common terms used in Agile software development.

## 1. Scrum Master

A **Scrum Master** is the person who ensures that the Scrum (Agile) process is followed correctly. They are **not the team's boss or manager**. Instead, they act as a **facilitator and coach**.

### Responsibilities
- Organizes Scrum meetings.
- Removes obstacles (blockers) that prevent the team from working.
- Ensures the team follows Agile and Scrum practices.
- Helps communication between developers, Product Owner, and stakeholders.
- Protects the team from unnecessary interruptions.

### Example
If a developer cannot access a database because of a permission issue, the Scrum Master works with the IT team to resolve it so the developer can continue working.

---

## 2. Scrum Call

A **Scrum Call** is any meeting conducted as part of the Scrum process. Examples include:

- Daily Stand-up (Daily Scrum)
- Sprint Planning
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective
- Backlog Refinement

When someone says,
> "Join the Scrum call at 10 AM."

they usually mean one of these Scrum meetings, most commonly the **Daily Stand-up**.

---

## 3. Stand-up Meeting (Daily Scrum)

A **Stand-up Meeting** is a short daily meeting held by the development team.

### Purpose
- Share progress.
- Identify blockers.
- Plan work for the day.

### Duration
- **15 minutes maximum**
- Usually held at the same time every day.

### Each team member answers three questions:

1. **What did I complete yesterday?**
2. **What will I work on today?**
3. **Is there any blocker preventing my work?**

### Example

**Developer A**
- Yesterday: Completed the login API.
- Today: Start implementing user authentication.
- Blocker: Waiting for database credentials.

**Developer B**
- Yesterday: Fixed UI bugs.
- Today: Integrate frontend with backend.
- Blocker: None.

The Scrum Master notes any blockers and works to resolve them after the meeting.

## How they relate

```
Scrum Framework
│
├── Scrum Master
│ └── Facilitates the Scrum process
│
├── Scrum Calls
│ ├── Daily Stand-up
│ ├── Sprint Planning
│ ├── Sprint Review
│ └── Sprint Retrospective
│
└── Development Team
 └── Attends meetings and develops the product
```

### In an internship or software company

A typical day might look like this:

- **9:30 AM** – Daily Stand-up (15 minutes)
- Team members discuss yesterday's work, today's plan, and blockers.
- Scrum Master records blockers.
- Developers continue coding.
- Throughout the day, the Scrum Master helps resolve issues.
- At the end of a sprint (usually 1–2 weeks), the team holds Sprint Review and Retrospective meetings.

If you're joining a software or AI internship, understanding these concepts will help you participate confidently in daily team meetings.

## Assistant

A **Scrum Team** is a small, self-managing group of people who work together to build and deliver a product in short development cycles called **Sprints**.

A Scrum Team consists of **three roles**:

### 1. Product Owner (PO)
The Product Owner decides **what should be built**.

**Responsibilities:**
- Understands customer and business requirements.
- Creates and prioritizes the Product Backlog (list of features and tasks).
- Clarifies requirements for the team.
- Accepts or rejects completed work.

**Example:**
"The application should allow users to log in using Google."

---

### 2. Scrum Master (SM)
The Scrum Master ensures the team follows Scrum practices and removes obstacles.

**Responsibilities:**
- Conducts Scrum meetings.
- Helps resolve blockers.
- Coaches the team on Agile principles.
- Improves team collaboration.

**Example:**
A developer cannot access the testing server. The Scrum Master coordinates with the infrastructure team to restore access.

---

### 3. Developers (Development Team)
These are the people who actually build the product.

Depending on the project, the team may include:
- Software Developers
- AI/ML Engineers
- Data Engineers
- QA/Test Engineers
- UI/UX Designers
- DevOps Engineers

**Responsibilities:**
- Design, develop, test, and deploy features.
- Collaborate with each other.
- Estimate and complete tasks during the Sprint.

## Characteristics of a Scrum Team

- Usually **10 or fewer people**.
- **Cross-functional**: The team has all the skills needed to deliver the product.
- **Self-managing**: Team members decide how to complete the work.
- Works in **Sprints** (typically 1–4 weeks).
- Delivers a working product increment at the end of each Sprint.

| Product Owner | Founder | Decides features such as PDF upload and chat support |

| AI Engineer | You | Build the RAG pipeline and integrate the LLM |
| Backend Developer | Developer A | Create APIs and database integration |
| Frontend Developer | Developer B | Build the chat interface |
| QA Engineer | Developer C | Test features and report bugs |

---

### Simple way to remember

- **Product Owner** → **What** should be built.
- **Scrum Master** → **How the team works together**.
- **Developers** → **Build the product**.

Together, these three roles form the **Scrum Team**, which collaborates to deliver working software at the end of every Sprint.
